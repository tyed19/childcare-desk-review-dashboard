Childcare Desk-Review Dashboard

A ranked review queue built from public licensing records for 1,320 childcare centers in Minnesota's seven-county Twin Cities metro and 13,297 facilities in California. The recommendations rank active Minnesota centers by how closely their licensing pattern resembles patterns documented in fraud cases that were caught — new licenses appearing at revoked providers' addresses, rapid multi-site scale-ups, addresses and phone numbers shared across different license holders.

Read this before using the queue

Every score on this page is a licensing-data sub-score: a maximum of 36 of the full model's 100 points. The other 64 points come from payment and claims data, which neither Minnesota nor California publishes by provider — so on the data available, no provider here can reach the model's Priority or Urgent tiers. A listing on this page is not a fraud finding. The only hard facts shown are documented state licensing actions: revocations, conditional licenses, fines, and violation histories taken from the states' own records.

1 · Operating where a revoked provider was

The strongest pattern in the model. Documented fraud schemes repeatedly re-emerged under new entities at the same addresses after enforcement action. Each center below is a different, currently active license now operating at the address of a provider the state revoked.

2 · Successor / rapid-scale patterns

New licenses that opened at a closed provider's site the day it closed, holders opening many licenses inside a short window, and brand-new licenses with large capacity — the scale-up shape seen in caught cases, and also the shape of ordinary acquisitions and franchise growth. Entries the file reads as same-brand license transfers are annotated as likely benign, so the reader can see the model telling the difference.

Back-tests: what this model would and would not have caught

Checked against cases that are already public record, so the limits of a licensing-only model stay visible next to its results.

Future Leaders Early Learning Center — Minneapolis, MN

The owner pleaded guilty in July 2026 to $4.6M in CCAP fraud plus $854K in nutrition-program fraud. The Minnesota House claims-by-site file shows $1,064,159 in Feeding Our Future claims for the site — payment data catches this pattern where it exists.

Licensing data alone would not have caught it. The catch required the payment layer this dashboard is missing.

Quality Learning Center — Minneapolis, MN

Received $1.9M in CCAP funds in FY2025. Its record shows a conditional license in 2022 and a state fine on June 12, 2024; the license closed January 6, 2026. Note: it has not been charged with fraud.

Licensing signals: yes. Fraud proof: no. It belongs in the "closed and cited, not charged" bucket — and this page keeps it there.

San Diego "ghost home" daycares — San Diego County, CA

Twelve people were charged in September 2026 in a scheme prosecutors put at $10M+, built on family home daycares where no children were present. Family homes are underrepresented in California's center file (the state counts 43,569 facilities; the center file holds 13,297), and "no kids present" appears in no licensing dataset.

This model would have missed them. A licensing layer cannot see an empty house.

The unlock: payment data by provider

Minnesota's January 2026 CCAP Provider Profile publishes counts by county and provider type, but not payments by provider — the 64 points that decide Priority and Urgent. The state can produce it: it gave one provider's FY2025 total ($1.9M) to CBS News on request.

Records requests are ready to file: MN DCYF for CCAP payments by provider FY2022–26, CA for San Diego County subsidy payments and the family-home licensing file, and bulk licensing-action histories including closed licenses. The moment one lands, the payment layer switches on.
